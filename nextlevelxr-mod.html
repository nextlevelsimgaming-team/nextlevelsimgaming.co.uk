NextLevelXR: the VR mod that spends your GPU where you actually look

18 October 2026 · updated 20 October 2026 for 1.4.3 · Gary Bowman

A standalone OpenXR mod from Next Level Sim Gaming and Elhydro, now with ShenronZero's helmet library. It trims the parts of the VR image you never look at in a cockpit and puts the saved GPU power back into a sharper picture. It doesn't use the OpenXR Toolkit at all, and replaces it.

What's new in 1.4.3

  • ShenronZero's helmets: ten real racing helmets, from open visor to Formula 1, with linings, visor tints, wear, glare, decals and scene lighting. More below.
  • Creator Mode for OBS: sends exactly what you see in the headset to OBS, with the whole helmet and the FPS panel. No mirror window to capture. More below.
  • A locked 90 with the FPS panel on: the panel and the settings checks now run off the game's render thread, so they cost the game nothing.
  • A better FPS panel: average FPS and 1% low, compact, standard and full layouts, and more positions.
  • Auto SS over a full lap, and an HTML report for every capture.
  • Fully digitally signed with Certum: the installer, the uninstaller, the mod, the app, the helmet module and every helper.

Tested by the team in iRacing, Le Mans Ultimate, F1 25, Assetto Corsa EVO, Assetto Corsa, Automobilista 2 and ATS / ETS2. The signed installer comes to the Discord's #downloads channel.

No OpenXR Toolkit. NextLevelXR replaces it.

NextLevelXR does not use the OpenXR Toolkit in any way. It's its own standalone OpenXR layer: you don't need the Toolkit installed, and none of its code is inside. For sim racing it's a complete replacement for what most of us used the Toolkit for, and it adds things the Toolkit never had.

  • FOV crop: every edge set on its own, both eyes always identical, plus visor categories by car type and per-edge margins.
  • Resolution and supersampling: the render size is cut to match the crop, sharpness is shown as "centre sharpness", and Auto SS finds what your GPU can hold.
  • Sharpening: Pop adds adaptive sharpening and mid-tone clarity, without halos.
  • FPS panel: FPS, average FPS, 1% low and CPU and GPU frame times in the headset, plus captures with a report.
  • New, not in the Toolkit: ShenronZero's helmets, Creator Mode for OBS, 3D Boost, and a Windows app to set it all per game.

NextLevelXR doesn't do upscaling (NIS / FSR), foveated rendering or hand tracking. If you rely on one of those, tell us on the Discord. Whatever you use, run only one FOV-cropping tool at a time, or the image gets cropped twice.

The problem: pixels you never see

Every VR headset renders a tall, wide picture for each eye. In a sim rig most of the top of that picture is roof lining and most of the bottom is your own knees, but the GPU still draws every pixel of it, every frame. That's frame time you could be spending on the track in front of you.

The edges are also the most expensive part. The way a VR image is projected packs pixels most densely at the edges, so cutting a little off the top and bottom saves far more work than you'd expect.

What NextLevelXR does

NextLevelXR is an OpenXR layer: it sits between the sim and your headset and quietly changes three things.

  1. It crops the field of view. You choose how far up, down, out and in the view goes. Each edge is set on its own and both eyes always get the same crop, mirrored.
  2. It shrinks the render size to match. The game is asked for a smaller image, worked out in the same flat projection it draws, so the frame really gets cheaper rather than just being masked.
  3. It puts the savings back into sharpness. Supersampling raises the resolution of what's left. Because the crop keeps the pixels in the middle of your view exactly as dense as native, sharpness is shown as simple "centre sharpness": 0% is native, +10% is sharper.

On a Pimax Dream Air SE with its example crop, the game renders about 47% fewer pixels before supersampling. With supersampling bringing the centre 9% sharper than native, it's still 36% fewer pixels than the headset's native picture. One racer on the Discord saw his GPU load drop from 95–100% to 50–60% after cropping.

Crop by the car you drive

An F1 car and a rally car don't need the same view. NextLevelXR's visor categories set the vertical view for the type of car, from a 25° slot for open-wheelers up to 60° for an open visor, with 45° for touring cars as the default. An offset of up to ±5° slides that window up or down for your seat height without changing the render size. With a helmet on, the crop follows the helmet's opening instead.

Auto SS: the most sharpness your GPU can hold

The right supersampling level depends on your GPU, the sim, the weather and how many cars are on track. So instead of guessing, NextLevelXR measures. Press Ctrl+F12 as you cross the start/finish line and again a lap later, and it times the GPU over the whole lap at your current settings. It then suggests the highest sharpness that fits about 90% of each frame, and one click saves it for that game. A full lap gives a safer answer than ten seconds on one straight.

ShenronZero's helmets

1.4.3 brings ShenronZero's full helmet library into NextLevelXR, replacing the old placeholder helmet. It puts a real racing helmet around your view: the shell, lining and visor are drawn for each eye and lit by the scene, so the helmet sits in the cockpit rather than on top of it.

  • Ten helmets, from an open visor to Formula 1, with lining colours.
  • Visors from clean to strong wear, eight tints and glare. Ctrl+F5 raises or lowers the visor while you drive.
  • Scene lighting and dynamic brightness, plus head inertia and car response in AC and AC EVO.
  • Sponsor decals across the top of the visor, and decal packs you can import.
  • Direct3D 11 and 12 games, including iRacing, AC, AMS2, LMU, ATS and ETS2.

The helmet is also the crop: with helmets on, NextLevelXR crops to the helmet's opening, so the pixels it hides are never rendered. And when you untick Show the helmet, the helmet module isn't loaded at all and costs nothing.

Creator Mode: record exactly what you see

Capturing one eye's view in OBS only ever showed part of the helmet, off to one side. Creator Mode fixes that: it draws a flat, centred 16:9 picture from the middle of your head, with the whole helmet and the FPS panel, and sends it straight to OBS. There's no mirror window on your desktop to capture, and it's off by default.

  1. Turn it on. In NextLevelXR Control, tick Send what I see to OBS.
  2. Add the source in OBS. Install Jabbah's OpenXR Mirror Capture plugin, add an OpenXR Mirror Capture source, and turn off Jabbah's own OpenXR layer.
  3. Record or stream. The app shows when OBS is receiving the picture.

3D Boost and Pop

3D Boost renders the game with your eyes slightly further apart, so the cockpit and track stand out in stronger 3D. It costs nothing, and the headset still gets your real eye positions. Pop adds adaptive sharpening and mid-tone clarity without halos, and switches itself completely off when you don't use it.

See what it's doing

A transparent panel in the headset shows FPS, average FPS and CPU and GPU frame times (Ctrl+F10). Ctrl+Shift+F10 switches between compact, standard and full layouts; the full one adds the 1% low, a live frame-time graph, missed frames, CPU and GPU usage and graphics memory. In 1.4.3 the panel runs off the game's render thread, so a 90 Hz headset holds 90 with it on.

Ctrl+F11 records every frame to a capture. Click it in the app and an HTML report opens with charts of every frame, the stutters and the lows, so you can compare settings lap for lap.

Which sims?

The team has tested 1.4.3 in iRacing, Le Mans Ultimate, F1 25, Assetto Corsa EVO, Assetto Corsa, Automobilista 2 and American and Euro Truck Simulator 2, so compatibility is no longer a concern. Native OpenXR sims work as they are; ATS and ETS2 need the -openxr launch option, and Assetto Corsa and AMS2 run through OpenComposite. The crop, helmets, Pop and the FPS panel work in Direct3D 11 and 12 games. Creator Mode works in Direct3D 11 games for now.

Fully digitally signed with Certum

Every file is signed with a Certum code-signing certificate and timestamped: the installer, the uninstaller, the NextLevelXR layer, the Control app, ShenronZero's helmet module and the helpers. Windows shows the publisher as Gary Bowman, and right-click > Properties > Digital Signatures lets anyone check. Any change to a signed file breaks its signature, so a tampered copy is easy to spot.
