What is the Pimax Crystal Pro?

The Crystal Pro is Pimax's new flagship PC VR headset, announced at the Pimax Frontier event on 6 October 2026. It pairs a 2880x2880 per eye QLED panel and Mini-LED local dimming with integrated eye tracking, and it is built around native foveated streaming. Pimax's pitch is that the GPU's native image gets sent wirelessly to the headset without being squeezed through a heavy compression step first.

It arrives alongside a new Ringless VR controller and a Spation mini-PC, covered further down. The official announcement is on the Pimax blog.

Native foveated streaming, explained

Foveated rendering means spending your GPU's effort where your eyes are actually looking and saving it everywhere else. The Crystal Pro's eye tracking makes that possible, and Pimax goes a step further with foveated streaming. For wireless play, the area you are looking at is sent at the highest quality while the periphery gets less, so the link carries detail where it matters.

For sim racing that could mean sharper dashboards, mirrors and braking markers without needing a monster GPU, if it works as described. That is the part I most want to test, because sim racing punishes any added latency or shimmer in the centre of your view.

Wi-Fi, 60G AirLink or wired?

You have three ways to connect the Crystal Pro to your PC.

  • Wi-Fi streaming is included with the headset and gives you a cable-free setup without extra hardware.
  • Third-generation 60G AirLink is the optional dedicated wireless link. Pimax quotes a 60GHz 2x2 MIMO connection of up to 6Gbps, under 2ms of added latency and 2880x2880 per eye at up to 90Hz. It needs a clear line of sight because 60GHz doesn't pass through walls.
  • DisplayPort 1.4a is the wired option, which Pimax's store lists at up to 120Hz.

If you drive from a fixed rig, a wired connection is usually the simplest choice, and the headset's wired support means you aren't forced into wireless. A 60G AirLink makes more sense if you want the cable gone and your PC sits in the same room with a clear line to the headset.

Also announced: Ringless VR Controllers and the Spation mini-PC

Ringless VR Controllers

Pimax's new controllers drop the tracking ring. They use hand tracking together with an IR-LED and a motion sensor for 6DOF tracking, and when you clip the pair together they work as a wireless gamepad. They have a TMR joystick, grip, trigger and menu buttons, linear haptics and a 710mAh battery rated for up to 40 hours.

Spation mini-PC

The Spation is a small form factor PC aimed at VR. The announced spec is a Ryzen 7 9800X3D with either a Radeon RX 9070 XT or a GeForce RTX 5070 Ti, 32GB of DDR5-6000, a 1TB NVMe drive and a 1000W SFX power supply. It has Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 5.4, 2.5G Ethernet, two DisplayPort 2.1 outputs, HDMI 2.1, a USB-C with DP 1.4a, one USB4 and six USB 3.2 Gen 2 ports. It runs Windows or SteamOS, and Pimax Play is coming to Linux.

Pimax also introduced a new brand colour, Stratosphere Blue, which you'll see across the new hardware.
