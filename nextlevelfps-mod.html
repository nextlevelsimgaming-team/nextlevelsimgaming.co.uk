NextLevelFPS: the fpsVR-style performance panel OpenXR never had

20 October 2026 · updated 31 October 2026 · Next Level Sim Gaming

A free, tiny OpenXR mod from Next Level Sim Gaming that puts a full performance dashboard in your headset, in any OpenXR title: FPS, frame times, a live graph, GPU temperature and power, lap captures and shareable reports. It draws the panel and changes nothing else about your picture.

NextLevelFPS in 20 seconds

  • fpsVR for OpenXR: the in-headset performance panel OpenXR users have been missing, in any OpenXR title.
  • GPU sensors on the panel: temperature, power draw and core clock, straight from the graphics driver.
  • Pick your rows: every row has its own switch, and the panel only takes the space of what you turn on.
  • Hotkeys on your wheel: click Bind, press any wheel or button box button, done.

  • GPU frame time is back in iRacing. iRacing draws the whole frame before it opens the OpenXR frame, so 1.0.2 had nothing to measure and hid the GPU row. 1.0.3 starts the GPU clock when the game takes its first image of the frame, so the GPU number and the purple graph line are back.

Why OpenXR needed this

For years the answer to “what’s my frame rate actually doing?” in VR was fpsVR — and fpsVR is SteamVR-only. The moment you switch to the native OpenXR route, the one most Pimax owners use for iRacing and the one modern sims are built around, that whole class of tool disappears. You can log numbers on the desktop, but nothing shows you, in the headset, mid-corner, whether that stutter was the CPU or the GPU.

NextLevelFPS is that tool for OpenXR. It installs as an OpenXR layer: it sits between the game and the headset, measures the real frames the game delivers, and draws one panel. That’s the whole job.

What’s on the panel

Three sizes, switched any time with Ctrl+Shift+F10: compact is just the FPS number, standard adds CPU and GPU frame times, and full is the dashboard pictured above. Seven positions, any background from transparent to solid, head-locked or fixed in the cockpit like part of the car.

  • FPS, CPU and GPU frame times, with the session average and 1% low.
  • A live frame-time graph with the frame budget marked and missed frames in red.
  • CPU and GPU usage and graphics memory.
  • GPU temperature, power draw and core clock, read from the NVIDIA driver’s own library — nothing extra to install. (AMD and Intel: those three rows stay hidden for now; everything else works.)
  • The time and your session length, so a long stint doesn’t swallow the evening.

Every row has its own switch in the Control app, and the panel grows and shrinks to exactly the rows you turn on. Just want FPS and the graph? That’s all the space it takes.

Put the hotkeys on your wheel

Show / hide (Ctrl+F10), switch layout (Ctrl+Shift+F10) and record (Ctrl+F11) all work in game, whichever window has focus. And every one of them can be a button on your wheel or button box instead: click Bind in the app, press the button, done. Wheel bindings go through Windows’ controller interface, so they land even when a sim swallows keyboard shortcuts.

Captures and reports

Press the record key at lights out and again at the flag. Every frame in between goes to a CSV — frame, CPU and GPU times, missed frames, and on NVIDIA cards the GPU temperature, power and clock, so you can see heat and throttling against your frame times. One click in the app turns a capture into a self-contained HTML report: average and 1% / 0.1% low FPS, stutter counts, an interactive timeline you can zoom, distributions and a statistics table. Drop the file in Discord and it opens for anyone.

Which sims?

Any OpenXR title: iRacing, Le Mans Ultimate, F1 25, Assetto Corsa EVO, Automobilista 2, ATS / ETS2, DCS, MSFS and the rest. Native OpenXR sims work as they are; ATS and ETS2 need the -openxr launch option, and Assetto Corsa and AMS2 run through OpenComposite. The panel draws in Direct3D 11 and 12 games — which covers every sim above.

Planning to run NextLevelXR too?

NextLevelFPS is the same panel that lives inside NextLevelXR (now in Early Access), as its own standalone mod for people who don’t need the FOV crop and supersampling. If you run both, turn the FPS panel on in only one of the two apps, or you’ll see two panels.

Fully digitally signed with Certum

The installer, the uninstaller, the NextLevelFPS layer and the Control app are all signed with a Certum code-signing certificate and timestamped. Windows shows the verified publisher, and right-click > Properties > Digital Signatures lets anyone check. Any change to a signed file breaks its signature, so a tampered copy is easy to spot — only trust builds from our own channels.
